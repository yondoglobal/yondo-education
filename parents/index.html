Questions worth asking before choosing a university

  • Why does this path fit my child, not just their grades?
  • What do graduates of this course do five years later?
  • What does daily life and support look like on campus?
  • How do the costs compare with realistic outcomes?
  • What happens if plans change midway?
  • Is my child ready, and how do we prepare them?

How families are part of it

  • Your view is heard

    Your counsellor talks with you separately about hopes, worries, budget and practical limits, recorded as the family’s view alongside your child’s own.

  • Permission comes first

    For students under 18, your counsellor confirms a parent or guardian’s permission before any answers are collected.

  • Privacy both ways

    Your child’s answers stay with the counsellor. A family summary is agreed with your child first, and what you share isn’t shown to your child without agreement.

  • Honest about money

    Tuition, living costs and outcomes are looked at side by side, and fees for our own support are explained before you decide anything.

  • One decision, together

    The consultation brings it together: possible directions, what each really involves, and what to test next.

  • You can message us too

    On your own or together with your child, on WhatsApp or by email.

The people behind Yondo

Harrsh Agrawal

Co-founder, Yondo

An education entrepreneur and operator who has worked across schools, coaching, student programmes and education ventures. He studied in India, Singapore and Dubai. Working with institutions and students, he has seen how much young people are capable of, and how little real exposure most of them get. Yondo is his attempt to close that gap.

Tushar Tayal

Co-founder, Yondo

An entrepreneur, global mobility professional and youth leader. He founded Travelidea, graduated as valedictorian from SP Jain School of Global Management, and secured All India Rank 1 in the CBSE AISSCE examination in 2019. His work keeps coming back to one problem: people have the ambition to move across borders, but not the access, guidance or pathways to do it well.
